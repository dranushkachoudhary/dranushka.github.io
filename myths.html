Dental Myths vs Facts

Not everything
you hear about teeth is true.

From scaling and cavities to wisdom teeth and whitening, dental advice is everywhere. Let's separate common myths from what your dentist actually wants you to know.

Simple dental education · by Dr. Anushka Choudhary, BDS

10
common dental
myths, explained.
Myth or fact?

Dental information can sometimes be confusing. These quick explanations are designed to help you understand common beliefs about oral health — without unnecessary fear or complicated terminology.

The myth check

What you've heard vs what matters

Here are some common dental beliefs patients often hear — along with a clearer explanation.

01
Scaling

“Scaling damages the enamel and makes teeth weak.”

Myth

Professional teeth cleaning removes a layer of the tooth and weakens it.

Fact

Professional scaling is designed to remove plaque and hardened calculus from the tooth surface. It does not intentionally remove healthy enamel.

02
Cavities

“A small cavity will heal by itself.”

Myth

If a cavity is small, brushing alone will make the hole disappear.

Fact

Very early mineral loss can sometimes be stopped or reversed. Once a true cavity or hole has formed, the lost tooth structure does not simply grow back.

03
Gum Health

“Bleeding gums mean I should stop brushing.”

Myth

Brushing makes the gums bleed, so avoiding brushing will allow them to heal.

Fact

Bleeding gums can be associated with plaque-related gum inflammation. Persistent bleeding should be assessed rather than simply avoiding oral hygiene.

04
Wisdom Teeth

“Everyone needs their wisdom teeth removed.”

Myth

Wisdom teeth should always be removed as soon as they appear.

Fact

Not everyone needs wisdom tooth removal. The decision depends on factors such as symptoms, position, disease and potential complications.

05
Sensitivity

“Sensitive teeth are always normal.”

Myth

Everyone gets sensitivity sometimes, so there is never a reason to have it checked.

Fact

Sensitivity can have several causes, including enamel wear, gum recession and dental problems. Persistent or worsening sensitivity deserves an evaluation.

06
Whitening

“Teeth whitening keeps teeth permanently white.”

Myth

Once teeth are professionally whitened, they stay that colour forever.

Fact

Whitening can lighten the colour of natural teeth, but the result is not necessarily permanent and varies between individuals.

07
Tooth Pain

“If my tooth doesn't hurt, there can't be a problem.”

Myth

Dental problems always cause noticeable pain from the beginning.

Fact

Some dental problems may have few or no obvious symptoms in their early stages. Regular dental examinations can help identify problems earlier.

08
Oral Hygiene

“Brushing harder means cleaner teeth.”

Myth

The harder you brush, the more effectively you remove plaque.

Fact

Effective brushing is about technique and consistency, not simply force. Excessive pressure can contribute to gum irritation and tooth wear.

09
Toothpaste

“More toothpaste means better cleaning.”

Myth

Covering the entire toothbrush with toothpaste gives a much better cleaning.

Fact

Using an appropriate amount of fluoride toothpaste along with good brushing technique matters more than using a large quantity.

10
Dental Visits

“I only need a dentist when something hurts.”

Myth

If your teeth feel fine, there is no reason to have them checked.

Fact

Dental visits can also help identify problems before they become painful or more complicated. The appropriate frequency depends on your individual oral-health needs.

One important reminder

A dental myth can sound convincing, especially when you've heard it many times. But your symptoms, dental history and examination matter more than a general rule found online. If something concerns you, speak with a qualified dental professional.
