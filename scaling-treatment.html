Dental Scaling

What dental scaling actually does, why your dentist may recommend it, and what you can expect before, during and after the procedure.

Dental scaling is a professional cleaning procedure used to remove plaque and hardened deposits such as calculus (tartar) from the surfaces of the teeth. Unlike regular brushing, professional cleaning can reach areas where a toothbrush cannot effectively remove hardened deposits.

What is dental scaling?

Plaque is a soft, sticky film that constantly forms on our teeth. When plaque is not removed effectively, it can harden into calculus, also called tartar.

Once calculus has formed, it cannot be properly removed with ordinary brushing or flossing at home. A dental professional uses specialised instruments to remove these deposits.

Scaling is therefore not simply about making the teeth look cleaner. It is an important part of maintaining healthy gums and controlling plaque and calculus buildup.

Why might you need dental scaling?

Your dentist or dental hygienist may recommend professional cleaning when there is visible plaque or calculus buildup, gum inflammation, or difficulty maintaining certain areas with routine home care.

Scaling may be particularly important when deposits have accumulated around or below the gumline, where they can contribute to gum disease.

The important distinction

Brushing and flossing help prevent plaque from building up. Professional scaling removes hardened deposits that have already formed. They work together — one does not replace the other.

What happens during dental scaling?

The exact procedure can vary depending on the amount and location of deposits and the health of your gums. Generally, your dentist examines your mouth first and then removes deposits using appropriate dental instruments.

1

Examination

Your dentist checks your teeth and gums and looks for plaque, calculus, inflammation and other oral health concerns.

2

Removal of deposits

Hardened deposits are carefully removed from the tooth surfaces using professional instruments.

3

Cleaning and polishing

Depending on your needs, the teeth may then be polished to help remove surface stains and leave the tooth surfaces smoother.

4

Home-care advice

Your dentist may recommend changes to brushing, flossing, interdental cleaning or other aspects of your oral hygiene.

Does dental scaling hurt?

Scaling is generally well tolerated, but some people may experience sensitivity or discomfort, particularly when the gums are inflamed or there is significant calculus buildup.

If you are uncomfortable during the procedure, let your dentist know. They can assess the situation and make the procedure as comfortable as possible.

What happens after scaling?

Your teeth may feel noticeably smoother after the deposits have been removed. Some people also notice temporary sensitivity, especially to cold foods or drinks.

If the gums were inflamed before treatment, mild bleeding or tenderness can sometimes occur. Your dentist can advise you about what is normal for your particular situation.

Good daily brushing and interdental cleaning are important after professional cleaning because plaque begins forming again naturally.

Does scaling make teeth loose?

Short answer: No — professional scaling does not make healthy teeth loose.

Sometimes people notice more space between teeth or increased mobility after large deposits are removed. This can happen because the deposits had been covering areas that were already affected by gum disease or loss of supporting tissues.

The scaling did not create that underlying problem. It removed the deposits that were hiding it.

Can scaling damage tooth enamel?

Professional scaling is designed to remove plaque and calculus from the teeth while preserving the tooth structure. Dental professionals use appropriate instruments and techniques according to the patient's needs.

If you have concerns about sensitivity or previous dental treatment, discuss them with your dentist before the procedure.

How often should you get your teeth professionally cleaned?

There is no single interval that is appropriate for everyone. The recommended frequency depends on your oral hygiene, gum health, calculus buildup, medical and dental history, and individual risk factors.

Some people may need professional cleaning more frequently, while others may require it less often. Your dentist can recommend an interval based on your individual needs.

When should you see a dentist?

Professional cleaning is only one part of dental care. You should arrange a dental examination if you notice persistent gum bleeding, swelling, pain, bad breath, tooth sensitivity, loose teeth or other changes in your mouth.

A dental examination can help identify the cause rather than simply treating the visible buildup.
