Tooth Discoloration

Why Are My Teeth Yellow? The Truth Behind Tooth Discoloration

“Doctor, my teeth are yellow. Do I need whitening?” It's a question dentists hear all the time. But before reaching for a whitening product, it's worth understanding why teeth look the way they do.

August 2026 · 6 min read · Dr. Anushka Choudhary, BDS

I'll start with something I wish more people knew: teeth are not naturally paper-white.

When patients sit in the dental chair and say, “My teeth are yellow,” they often say it as though something has gone terribly wrong.

But tooth colour is naturally different from person to person. The appearance of a tooth comes from the interaction between its enamel and the dentin underneath it. Dentin has a naturally yellowish colour, while enamel is partially translucent.

So sometimes, what looks like “yellow teeth” is simply your natural tooth colour — not poor hygiene and not necessarily a sign that something is wrong.

So why do teeth become darker or more yellow?

There isn't one single answer. Tooth discoloration can come from stains on the outside of the tooth, changes within the tooth itself, or a combination of both.

1. Coffee, tea and other strongly coloured foods

Coffee, tea, cola, red wine and other deeply pigmented foods and drinks can contribute to surface staining over time. These are called extrinsic stains because they are associated with the outer surface of the tooth.

Tobacco use can also contribute significantly to tooth discoloration.

2. Your enamel changes as you age

Enamel becomes thinner and more translucent with age. As that happens, the naturally yellowish dentin underneath can become more visible.

In other words, sometimes teeth look more yellow not because they have become “dirty,” but because the structure of the tooth has changed over time.

3. Plaque and tartar can affect how teeth look

When plaque isn't removed regularly, it can contribute to staining and discoloration. Hardened plaque, known as tartar or calculus, cannot simply be brushed away at home and may make teeth appear darker around the gumline.

This is one reason professional cleaning can make a noticeable difference for some people — particularly when the problem is surface deposits rather than the natural colour of the tooth.

4. Some discoloration comes from inside the tooth

Not every stain can be removed by brushing or professional cleaning.

Intrinsic discoloration occurs within the tooth and can have several causes, including certain medications during tooth development, fluorosis, dental trauma, some dental conditions, and changes associated with the tooth's internal tissues.

This is why simply buying a stronger whitening toothpaste isn't always the answer.

A common misconception

“Whiter teeth = healthier teeth.”

Not necessarily. Tooth colour and tooth health are different things. A naturally slightly yellow tooth can be perfectly healthy, while a very white-looking tooth isn't automatically healthier.

What about whitening?

Whitening can be useful when someone wants to change the colour of their natural teeth for cosmetic reasons. Depending on the cause of discoloration, whitening may help with certain surface and intrinsic stains.

But the right treatment depends on why the teeth are discoloured in the first place.

A dental examination is particularly important when one tooth suddenly changes colour, when discoloration is associated with pain or trauma, or when you aren't sure what is causing the change.

And please be careful with DIY whitening

Social media has made “natural whitening” hacks incredibly popular — lemon, vinegar, charcoal, baking soda mixtures, and other home remedies.

The problem is that something being natural doesn't automatically make it safe for enamel.

The American Dental Association notes that evidence for many DIY whitening methods is limited, and some abrasive approaches can damage tooth surfaces rather than safely whitening them.

So what should you actually do?

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between your teeth regularly.
  • If you regularly drink coffee or tea, rinse with water afterward.
  • Avoid tobacco use.
  • Don't use aggressive DIY whitening methods.
  • If you're considering whitening, talk to your dentist first so the cause of discoloration can be identified.

One last thing I'd like you to remember

The goal isn't necessarily to have the whitest teeth in the room.

The goal is to have healthy teeth that you're comfortable smiling with.

Sometimes that means professional cleaning. Sometimes it means whitening. And sometimes, it simply means understanding that your natural tooth colour is completely okay.
