Dental Treatment Guide

Gum treatment,
explained simply.

Healthy gums are an important part of a healthy mouth. Gum problems can develop gradually and may sometimes cause few noticeable symptoms in the early stages. Understanding gum disease and its treatment can help you recognise when professional dental care may be needed.

Understanding gum health, gum inflammation and professional gum care.
Understanding the problem

What is gum disease?

Gum disease refers to inflammation and infection affecting the tissues that surround and support the teeth.

It can range from early gum inflammation, commonly known as gingivitis, to more advanced periodontal disease where the deeper supporting tissues around the teeth are affected.

Dental plaque, a sticky layer containing bacteria, plays an important role in the development of gum inflammation. If plaque is not removed effectively, it can contribute to irritation and inflammation of the gums.

What to look for

Common signs of gum problems

Gum problems can appear differently from person to person. Some common signs include:

  • Bleeding while brushing or cleaning between the teeth.
  • Red, swollen or tender gums.
  • Persistent bad breath.
  • Gums that appear to be pulling away from the teeth.
  • Increased tooth sensitivity.
  • Changes in the way teeth feel or fit together.
  • Loose teeth in more advanced cases.
Important

Bleeding gums are not something you should simply accept.

Persistent bleeding can be a sign of gum inflammation. It is worth having the cause assessed rather than simply stopping brushing or avoiding the area.

Treatment

How are gum problems treated?

Gum treatment depends on the cause and severity of the problem. Early gum inflammation may often be managed through improved oral hygiene, professional cleaning and appropriate follow-up.

When periodontal disease has progressed deeper around the teeth, additional professional treatment may be required to remove deposits and control inflammation.

Your dentist may assess the gums, measure periodontal pockets when appropriate, check for plaque and calculus and evaluate the supporting tissues before deciding on the most suitable treatment.

Professional care

Why might professional cleaning be recommended?

Brushing and cleaning between the teeth are essential for daily plaque control. However, hardened deposits such as calculus cannot be reliably removed with a toothbrush at home.

Professional dental cleaning allows deposits to be removed from areas that may be difficult to clean effectively during everyday oral hygiene.

Remember

Professional cleaning and good home care work together.

Cleaning can remove accumulated deposits, but maintaining effective daily plaque control is important for keeping the gums healthy over time.

What to expect

What may happen during a gum assessment?

1

Examination

Your dentist examines the teeth and gums and looks for signs of inflammation, plaque, calculus and other changes.

2

Gum assessment

Depending on the situation, the dentist may assess the depth of the gum spaces around the teeth and check the supporting tissues.

3

Professional cleaning

If plaque or calculus is present, professional cleaning may be recommended to remove deposits that cannot be adequately removed at home.

4

Home-care guidance

Your dentist or dental professional may explain brushing technique and recommend suitable ways to clean between your teeth.

5

Follow-up

Further appointments may be recommended depending on the condition of the gums and how they respond to treatment.

Prevention

How can you protect your gums every day?

Daily plaque control is one of the most important parts of maintaining healthy gums.

  • Brush your teeth twice a day using an appropriate technique.
  • Clean between your teeth regularly.
  • Attend regular dental examinations.
  • Follow professional cleaning recommendations when advised.
  • Do not ignore persistent bleeding, swelling or gum changes.
Common questions

Gum treatment: quick answers

Is bleeding while brushing normal?

Persistent bleeding is not something that should simply be ignored. It can be associated with gum inflammation and should be assessed if it continues.

Can gum disease be treated?

Gum problems can often be managed effectively when they are identified and treated appropriately. Treatment depends on the type and severity of the condition.

Can brushing alone remove calculus?

No. Brushing can help control plaque, but hardened calculus generally requires professional removal.

Can gum disease cause loose teeth?

Advanced periodontal disease can damage the tissues and bone supporting the teeth and may contribute to tooth mobility.

Should I stop brushing if my gums bleed?

In general, stopping oral hygiene around a bleeding area is not the solution. Persistent bleeding should be discussed with a dental professional so the cause can be identified and appropriate care recommended.

Remember

Healthy gums are part of a healthy smile.

If you notice persistent bleeding, swelling, bad breath, gum recession, sensitivity or loose teeth, consider having your gums assessed by a dental professional. Early attention can help identify problems before they become more advanced.
