Gum Treatment

Healthy gums
support healthy teeth.

Gum problems are common and can sometimes develop gradually without causing significant discomfort. Understanding what happens to the gums and how gum problems are treated can help you recognise the importance of professional dental care.

Understanding the problem

What is gum disease?

Gum disease refers to inflammation and infection affecting the tissues that surround and support the teeth. It can range from early gum inflammation to more advanced disease involving deeper supporting tissues.

Plaque, a sticky layer of bacteria that forms on the teeth, plays an important role in the development of gum problems. If plaque is not removed effectively, it can contribute to inflammation of the gums.

What to look for

Common signs of gum problems

Gum problems can present in different ways. Some common signs include:

  • Bleeding while brushing or flossing
  • Red, swollen or tender gums
  • Persistent bad breath
  • Gums that appear to be pulling away from the teeth
  • Increased tooth sensitivity
  • Loose teeth in more advanced cases
Treatment

How are gum problems treated?

Treatment depends on the severity and cause of the gum problem. Early gum inflammation may often be managed with improved oral hygiene and professional dental cleaning.

When gum disease has progressed deeper around the teeth, additional professional treatment may be required to remove deposits and control the infection and inflammation.

Treatment is not always the same for everyone.

A dentist may examine the gums, check for plaque and calculus, assess the supporting tissues and determine the appropriate treatment based on the individual situation.

Professional care

Why professional cleaning may be recommended

Brushing and flossing are essential for daily plaque control, but hardened deposits such as calculus cannot always be removed effectively at home.

Professional cleaning allows a dental professional to remove deposits from areas that may be difficult to clean with regular home care.

Prevention

Protecting your gums every day

Maintaining good oral hygiene is an important part of preventing and controlling gum problems.

  • Brush your teeth twice a day with appropriate technique.
  • Clean between your teeth regularly.
  • Have regular dental check-ups.
  • Follow professional cleaning recommendations when advised.
  • Do not ignore persistent gum bleeding or swelling.
Important to remember

Bleeding gums should not simply be ignored.

Occasional bleeding can have different causes, but persistent bleeding while brushing or flossing can be a sign of gum inflammation and deserves attention.

Early evaluation can help identify the cause and allow appropriate care before a gum problem becomes more advanced.

Still wondering?

Have a question about your gums?

If you are experiencing bleeding, swelling, sensitivity or another gum-related concern, you can ask your question directly.
