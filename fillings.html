Treatment Guide

Dental fillings,
explained simply.

A dental filling is one of the most common treatments used to repair a tooth affected by decay or certain types of damage. Here's what the treatment actually involves and what you can expect.

Understanding the treatment

What is a dental filling?

A dental filling is a restorative treatment used to repair a tooth that has been damaged, most commonly because of dental decay.

During the treatment, the dentist removes the damaged or decayed portion of the tooth and then fills the resulting space with a suitable restorative material.

The goal: remove the damaged portion of the tooth and restore its shape, function and integrity as much as possible.

Why it may be recommended

Why do you need a filling?

Fillings are most commonly recommended when a tooth has a cavity caused by dental decay.

Depending on the situation, restorative treatment may also be considered for certain forms of tooth wear, fractures or other defects.

A filling may help:

  • Restore a tooth affected by decay.
  • Restore lost tooth structure.
  • Improve the tooth's shape and function.
  • Protect the remaining tooth structure.
During the appointment

What happens during a filling?

The exact procedure varies depending on the tooth, the extent of the decay and the type of restoration being placed.

1. Examination

Your dentist examines the tooth and may use dental X-rays when appropriate to understand how extensive the problem is.

2. Local anaesthesia

If necessary, local anaesthesia is used to numb the area so that the procedure can be performed comfortably.

3. Removing the damaged tooth structure

The dentist carefully removes the decayed or damaged portion of the tooth.

4. Cleaning and preparing the tooth

The area is cleaned and prepared so that the restorative material can be placed appropriately.

5. Placing the filling

The filling material is placed into the prepared area and shaped to restore the tooth.

6. Checking the bite

Your dentist checks how your teeth come together and makes adjustments if necessary.

A common concern

Does getting a filling hurt?

Many people worry that getting a filling will be painful. The treatment itself is generally performed with local anaesthesia when needed, which numbs the treatment area.

You may feel pressure, vibration or movement during the procedure, but you should not simply have to tolerate significant pain. If you feel discomfort during treatment, tell your dentist so they can address it.

Remember: the experience can vary depending on the tooth, the extent of the decay, sensitivity and individual factors.

After the treatment

What happens after a filling?

It is possible to experience some temporary sensitivity after a filling, particularly to hot, cold or pressure.

Your dentist may give you specific instructions depending on the type of filling and the condition of the tooth.

If sensitivity is severe, persistent or worsening, it is a good idea to contact your dentist for an assessment.

An important distinction

Is a filling always enough?

No. The appropriate treatment depends on how much of the tooth has been affected and whether the pulp inside the tooth is involved.

A smaller area of decay may be treatable with a filling, while a more extensively damaged tooth may require another form of treatment.

In some situations, a tooth may require root canal treatment followed by a suitable restoration.

Protecting your teeth

Can you prevent cavities?

Good daily oral hygiene and regular dental care can help reduce the risk of tooth decay.

  • Brush your teeth regularly with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between your teeth regularly.
  • Limit frequent exposure to sugary foods and drinks.
  • Attend dental check-ups as recommended for you.
  • Do not ignore persistent tooth sensitivity or pain.
Common questions

Dental fillings: FAQs

How long does a filling take?

The time depends on the tooth, the extent of the problem and the type of filling being placed. Your dentist can tell you approximately how long your particular appointment is likely to take.

Can a filled tooth get a cavity again?

Yes. A tooth that has been filled can develop decay again, including around a restoration. Good oral hygiene and regular dental care remain important.

Does a filling last forever?

No restoration lasts forever. How long a filling remains functional depends on factors such as the material used, the tooth, chewing forces, oral hygiene and other individual factors.

Should I get a filling if my tooth doesn't hurt?

Tooth decay does not always cause pain in its early stages. If your dentist has identified decay, treatment may be recommended even when the tooth feels completely normal.
