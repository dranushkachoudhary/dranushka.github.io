Dental Treatment Guide

Dental Implants,
explained simply.

Dental implants are one option for replacing missing teeth. They are designed to provide support for replacement teeth and can help restore function and appearance in suitable patients.

Understanding the treatment

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small component, usually made from a biocompatible material such as titanium, that is placed into the jawbone to provide support for a replacement tooth.

Once the implant has integrated with the surrounding bone, it can be used to support a dental crown, bridge or other appropriate restoration.

Why might they be recommended?

Why are dental implants used?

Dental implants may be considered when one or more teeth are missing and a patient is looking for a fixed replacement option.

  • To replace a single missing tooth.
  • To support a bridge when several teeth are missing.
  • To support certain types of dentures.
  • To restore chewing function and appearance.
The general process

How does implant treatment work?

Implant treatment is usually completed in stages. The exact treatment plan varies depending on the patient's oral health, bone condition and the type of restoration required.

Step 01

Examination & Planning

Your dentist evaluates the mouth, remaining teeth, gums and jawbone. Imaging may also be used to plan the procedure.

Step 02

Implant Placement

The implant is placed into the jawbone under appropriate local anaesthesia or other planned anaesthesia.

Step 03

Healing & Integration

The implant is allowed time to integrate with the surrounding bone before the final restoration is placed.

Step 04

Final Tooth

A custom-made restoration, such as a crown, may then be attached to the implant.

Is everyone suitable?

Can anyone get a dental implant?

Dental implants are not automatically suitable for every patient. Your dentist needs to assess factors such as the health of the gums, available bone, oral hygiene, general health and the condition of the surrounding teeth.

In some situations, additional procedures may be required before implant placement. Your dentist can explain whether an implant is an appropriate option in your particular case.

A common concern

Does getting an implant hurt?

Implant placement is performed using appropriate anaesthesia, so you should not feel the surgical procedure itself. Some soreness, swelling or discomfort can occur afterward as the tissues heal. Your dentist will provide instructions for managing recovery.

After treatment

What happens after an implant?

Good oral hygiene and regular dental visits remain important after implant treatment. Although implants cannot develop tooth decay like natural teeth, the tissues around them can still develop inflammation or disease.

Following your dentist's cleaning and maintenance advice can help protect the implant and surrounding tissues over time.

Important to remember

An implant is not simply a replacement tooth.

Successful implant treatment involves the implant, surrounding bone and gums, and the final restoration. Careful planning and long-term maintenance are important for a good outcome.
