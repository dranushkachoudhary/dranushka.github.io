Dental Treatments

Dental treatments,
explained simply.

Dental treatment names can sound complicated. Explore common procedures below to understand what they are generally used for, what they involve, and what you can expect.

Preventive Dentistry

Dental Scaling

Professional cleaning that removes plaque, tartar and deposits that regular brushing cannot remove completely.

Commonly used for:
Tartar buildup, gum inflammation and maintaining gum health.
Learn About Scaling →

Restorative Dentistry

Dental Fillings

A filling restores a tooth after decay or certain types of damage by replacing the affected tooth structure.

Commonly used for:
Dental cavities and certain small areas of tooth damage.
Learn About Fillings →
🦷
Endodontic Treatment

Root Canal Treatment

A procedure used to treat disease or infection involving the pulp inside a tooth.

Commonly used for:
Deep decay, pulp inflammation or infection involving the tooth.
Learn About Root Canal →
👑
Restorative Dentistry

Dental Crowns

A crown is a custom-made restoration that covers and protects a tooth when substantial restoration is needed.

Commonly used for:
Heavily damaged, weakened or root-canal-treated teeth.
Learn About Crowns →
🦷
Oral Surgery

Tooth Extraction

Removal of a tooth may sometimes be recommended when a tooth cannot be predictably restored or other factors make removal necessary.

Commonly considered for:
Severely damaged teeth, certain infections or impacted teeth.
Learn About Extraction →

Cosmetic Dentistry

Teeth Whitening

Whitening treatments are designed to lighten the colour of natural teeth and reduce certain types of staining.

Commonly used for:
Certain external and internal tooth discoloration.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Dental Veneers

Thin restorations placed on the front surface of selected teeth to change aspects of their appearance.

May be considered for:
Certain cosmetic concerns involving tooth shape, colour or proportions.

Gum Treatment

Deep Cleaning

A deeper form of periodontal cleaning used when deposits and inflammation affect areas below the gumline.

May be recommended for:
Certain cases of gum disease and periodontal inflammation.

Wisdom Teeth

Wisdom Tooth Removal

Wisdom teeth sometimes require removal when they cause problems or are unlikely to function properly.

May be considered for:
Impacted teeth, recurrent infection, damage to nearby teeth or other problems.

Before Treatment

The right treatment depends on the right diagnosis.

Two people can have similar symptoms but need completely different treatments. A dental examination, medical and dental history and, when appropriate, diagnostic tests help a dentist determine what treatment is suitable.

One important thing

Treatment isn't one-size-fits-all.

These pages are educational guides designed to help you understand common dental procedures. They are not a recommendation for any specific treatment. Your dentist can explain which options are appropriate for your individual situation.
