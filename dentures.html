Dentures

Replacing missing teeth,
simply explained.

Missing teeth can affect chewing, speech and confidence. Dentures are one option for replacing missing teeth and restoring some of the functions of a natural smile.

Understanding dentures

What are dentures?

Dentures are removable dental appliances designed to replace missing teeth and, depending on the type, some of the surrounding oral structures.

They can be used to replace several missing teeth or, in some situations, all of the teeth in one or both arches.

Different options

What types of dentures are available?

The type of denture recommended depends on the number of missing teeth, the condition of the remaining teeth, the gums and jawbone, and individual needs.

  • Complete dentures can replace all teeth in an arch.
  • Partial dentures can replace some missing teeth when other natural teeth remain.
  • Implant-supported dentures use dental implants to provide additional support and stability in suitable patients.
When they may be considered

When might dentures be recommended?

Dentures may be considered when multiple teeth are missing or when remaining teeth cannot be predictably maintained.

They can provide a removable way to replace missing teeth and may help improve chewing, speech and appearance.

Dentures are not the only option.

Depending on the individual situation, other tooth replacement options such as bridges or dental implants may also be considered.

Getting used to dentures

What is it like to wear dentures?

New dentures can take some time to get used to. During the adjustment period, some people may notice changes in speech, chewing or how the appliance feels in the mouth.

With time and practice, many people become more comfortable using their dentures. Follow-up visits may sometimes be needed to improve fit and comfort.

Denture care

How should dentures be cared for?

Proper care helps keep dentures clean and comfortable and also supports good oral hygiene.

  • Clean dentures regularly as advised by your dentist.
  • Handle them carefully to avoid dropping or damaging them.
  • Keep the mouth and any remaining natural teeth clean.
  • Follow instructions regarding how and where to store the dentures when they are not being worn.
  • Attend dental check-ups to assess the fit and condition of the dentures and oral tissues.
Important to remember

A denture should be comfortable and properly fitted.

Dentures that become loose, uncomfortable or damaged may need professional assessment. The shape of the gums and supporting tissues can change over time, which may affect how a denture fits.

If you experience persistent soreness, difficulty chewing or a change in fit, speak with your dentist rather than continuing to use an uncomfortable appliance.
