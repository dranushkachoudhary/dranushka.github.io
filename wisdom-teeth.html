Dental Treatment Guide

Wisdom Teeth,
explained simply.

Wisdom teeth are the last set of molars to develop. They do not always cause problems or need to be removed, but their position and condition can sometimes lead to pain, infection or other dental concerns.

Understanding wisdom teeth

What are wisdom teeth?

Wisdom teeth are the third molars located at the very back of the mouth. They are usually the last permanent teeth to develop and commonly appear during the late teenage years or early adulthood.

Some people have enough space for their wisdom teeth to erupt normally and function like other teeth. Others may have teeth that remain partially or completely trapped beneath the gum or bone.

An important point

Not every wisdom tooth needs to be removed.

A wisdom tooth may not require removal if it is healthy, properly positioned, functional, easy to clean and not causing problems. Your dentist evaluates the individual tooth and surrounding tissues before recommending treatment.

When problems occur

What problems can wisdom teeth cause?

Problems are more likely when a wisdom tooth does not have enough room to erupt properly or becomes difficult to clean.

  • Pain or discomfort at the back of the mouth.
  • Swelling or inflammation around the tooth.
  • Infection of the surrounding gum tissue.
  • Food becoming trapped around a partially erupted tooth.
  • Tooth decay affecting the wisdom tooth or neighbouring tooth.
  • Difficulty cleaning the area properly.
  • Damage or pressure affecting nearby structures in some cases.
Impacted wisdom teeth

What does "impacted" mean?

An impacted wisdom tooth is one that cannot erupt into its normal position because it is blocked by bone, gum tissue, another tooth or its own position within the jaw.

An impacted tooth may be completely covered by gum or bone, or it may only partially emerge into the mouth.

Treatment decisions

When might wisdom tooth removal be recommended?

Removal may be considered when a wisdom tooth is causing significant or recurring problems or when examination shows that treatment is necessary.

  • Repeated infections or inflammation.
  • Significant or persistent pain.
  • Tooth decay that cannot be predictably managed.
  • Gum disease around the wisdom tooth or neighbouring tooth.
  • Damage to an adjacent tooth.
  • Certain cysts or other pathological changes.

The decision depends on the individual situation and should be made after appropriate clinical examination and imaging when required.

Before treatment

How is a wisdom tooth assessed?

Your dentist will usually examine the area and ask about symptoms. Dental X-rays may be recommended to understand the position of the wisdom tooth, its roots and its relationship with nearby structures.

This information helps the dentist decide whether the tooth can be monitored, treated or should be considered for removal.

If removal is needed

What happens during wisdom tooth removal?

The exact procedure depends on whether the tooth has erupted normally or is impacted.

Step 01

Examination

The dentist examines the tooth and reviews appropriate dental imaging.

Step 02

Anaesthesia

Local anaesthesia is commonly used so the area is numb during the procedure.

Step 03

Tooth Removal

Depending on the tooth's position, removal may be straightforward or may require a minor surgical procedure.

Step 04

Recovery

Your dentist will provide instructions for caring for the area while it heals.

A common concern

Does wisdom tooth removal hurt?

The area is numbed with appropriate anaesthesia during the procedure, so you should not feel the tooth being removed itself. Some soreness, swelling or discomfort can occur afterward as the tissues heal.

After removal

What should you expect during recovery?

Some swelling, tenderness and difficulty opening the mouth can occur after wisdom tooth removal, particularly after surgical removal of an impacted tooth.

Your dentist will give you specific instructions regarding eating, cleaning the area, medications and follow-up care.

If you experience symptoms that concern you during recovery, contact your treating dentist for advice.

Remember

Wisdom teeth are not automatically a problem.

Some wisdom teeth remain healthy and functional without causing problems. Others may require monitoring or treatment. The right approach depends on the position and condition of the tooth and the health of the surrounding tissues.
