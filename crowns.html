Dental Treatment Guide

Dental crowns,
explained simply.

A dental crown is a restoration that covers a tooth to help restore its shape, function and strength. Here's why a crown may be recommended, what happens during treatment and what you can expect afterward.

Understanding the treatment

What is a dental crown?

A dental crown is a custom-made restoration designed to cover a damaged, weakened or heavily restored tooth.

It fits over the visible portion of the tooth and is designed to restore its shape, function and appearance.

Crowns can be made from different materials, and the appropriate choice depends on factors such as the tooth, its location and the clinical situation.

Why might you need one?

Why are dental crowns recommended?

A crown may be considered when a tooth has lost a significant amount of structure and needs additional protection or support.

They may also be used in certain situations after root canal treatment, depending on the condition and remaining structure of the tooth.

A crown is not automatically required for every damaged or root-treated tooth. The decision depends on the individual tooth.

An important point

A crown protects a tooth — it doesn't make a weak tooth "new."

A crown can provide coverage and protection, but the underlying natural tooth remains important. Good oral hygiene and regular dental care are still necessary.

During treatment

What happens when you get a crown?

1

Examination and planning

Your dentist evaluates the tooth and determines whether a crown is appropriate.

2

Tooth preparation

The tooth is carefully prepared to create the required space and shape for the crown.

3

Impression or digital scan

An impression or digital scan may be taken so the crown can be designed to fit the tooth and surrounding teeth.

4

Temporary protection

Depending on the treatment process, a temporary restoration may be placed while the final crown is being made.

5

Final crown placement

The final crown is checked for fit, appearance and bite before being permanently placed when appropriate.

A common concern

Does getting a crown hurt?

The tooth is generally numbed with local anaesthesia during preparation, so the procedure is intended to be comfortable.

You may experience pressure, vibration or sensitivity around the tooth during or after treatment.

If you experience significant or persistent pain, contact your dentist so the tooth can be assessed.

After treatment

What can you expect afterward?

Some temporary sensitivity or gum tenderness can occur following crown treatment.

Your dentist may give you specific instructions regarding eating, cleaning and caring for the tooth.

A crowned tooth still needs regular brushing, cleaning between the teeth and routine dental care.

Common questions

Dental crowns: quick answers

Does every root canal-treated tooth need a crown?

Not necessarily. The need for a crown depends on factors such as the amount of remaining tooth structure, the tooth involved and the forces it experiences.

How long does a dental crown last?

The lifespan of a crown varies depending on factors such as the material, oral hygiene, bite forces and general care. Regular dental check-ups can help identify problems early.

Can a crowned tooth get a cavity?

Yes. The natural tooth underneath and around a crown can still develop decay, particularly if plaque accumulates around the crown margins.

Can a crown come off?

A crown can occasionally become loose or dislodged. If this happens, avoid trying to permanently fix it yourself and contact your dentist for assessment.

Remember

A crown is only one part of protecting a tooth.

Even after receiving a crown, maintaining good oral hygiene, managing your diet, avoiding damaging habits and attending regular dental examinations remain important for the health of the tooth and surrounding tissues.
