Why Do My Teeth Hurt When I Drink Something Cold?

That sudden sharp pain from cold water, ice cream or even a breath of cold air is something many people experience. But why does it happen — and when should you be concerned?

DR. ANUSHKA CHOUDHARY, BDS · AUGUST 2026 · 6 MIN READ

What is tooth sensitivity?

Tooth sensitivity is usually experienced as a short, sharp sensation when your teeth come into contact with something cold, hot, sweet or sometimes even acidic.

You might notice it while drinking cold water, eating ice cream, having a hot cup of tea or brushing your teeth.

The important clue is often the pattern: sensitivity is typically sudden and brief rather than a pain that continues for a long time after the trigger is removed.

Why can teeth become sensitive?

A tooth has a hard outer layer called enamel. Underneath it is dentin, which contains tiny pathways that can transmit sensations toward the nerve inside the tooth.

When dentin becomes exposed or the protective layers of the tooth are reduced, external stimuli can sometimes reach the sensitive tissues more easily.

Common reasons include:

  • Gum recession exposing part of the tooth root.
  • Tooth wear caused by aggressive brushing or other factors.
  • Enamel erosion associated with frequent acidic foods or drinks.
  • Tooth decay or a damaged tooth.
  • Sensitivity after certain dental procedures.
  • Cracked or fractured teeth in some situations.

Is tooth sensitivity always a problem?

Not necessarily. Some people experience occasional sensitivity, particularly after certain dental treatments or when they have exposed tooth surfaces.

However, persistent or worsening sensitivity shouldn't simply be ignored because several different dental problems can produce similar symptoms.

Common misconception

“My teeth are sensitive, so I should stop brushing that area.”

Avoiding brushing completely isn't the solution. Good oral hygiene remains important. If brushing is uncomfortable, discuss your symptoms with a dentist so the underlying cause can be identified.

What can you do about sensitive teeth?

The right approach depends on the cause. Some simple habits can help reduce irritation while you arrange a dental evaluation if needed.

  • Use a soft-bristled toothbrush.
  • Brush gently rather than applying excessive pressure.
  • Maintain regular brushing and flossing.
  • Consider a toothpaste formulated for sensitive teeth.
  • Limit frequent exposure to highly acidic foods and drinks.
  • Don't ignore sensitivity that keeps returning or becomes worse.

When should you see a dentist?

It is a good idea to have persistent or unexplained sensitivity evaluated, particularly if it affects one specific tooth.

You should seek dental attention if sensitivity is accompanied by symptoms such as:

  • Pain that continues after the hot or cold trigger is removed.
  • Spontaneous tooth pain.
  • Pain when biting or chewing.
  • Swelling around the tooth or gums.
  • A visible crack, fracture or damaged filling.
  • Symptoms that are becoming increasingly frequent or severe.

One important point: sensitivity is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Two people can experience very similar sensitivity for completely different reasons.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do my teeth hurt when I drink cold water?

Cold temperatures can trigger sensitivity when dentin or other sensitive tooth surfaces are exposed. Tooth wear, gum recession, decay and other conditions can contribute.

Can sensitive teeth become normal again?

Sometimes sensitivity can improve when the underlying cause is addressed. The outlook depends on why the sensitivity is occurring.

Is sensitive toothpaste helpful?

Toothpastes designed for sensitivity can help some people reduce symptoms when used consistently. However, they don't replace a dental examination when there may be an underlying dental problem.

Why is only one tooth sensitive?

Sensitivity limited to one tooth can have several possible causes, including decay, a damaged restoration, a crack or changes around that particular tooth. A dentist can determine the cause clinically.

Should I avoid cold foods completely?

You don't necessarily need to avoid them permanently. If cold foods consistently trigger pain, however, it is worth understanding why the sensitivity is occurring.
