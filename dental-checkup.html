Dental Check-Up Explained

Your Dental Check-Up,
explained simply.

What actually happens during a dental check-up, what your dentist looks for, when X-rays may be needed, and why regular dental examinations matter even when nothing hurts.

Dentistry · Patient Education · August 2026

A dental check-up is more than simply looking at your teeth. It gives your dentist an opportunity to assess your teeth, gums, bite, oral tissues and overall oral health and identify problems that may not yet be causing symptoms.

What is a dental check-up?

A dental check-up is a clinical examination of your mouth performed by a dentist.

During the appointment, your dentist may examine your teeth and gums, ask about symptoms or concerns, review your dental history and determine whether any further investigations or treatment are appropriate.

Depending on your individual situation, the appointment may also include professional cleaning or other preventive care.

No pain does not always mean no problem.

Some dental problems can develop quietly before they cause noticeable pain. Regular examinations can help identify concerns earlier.

Why are regular dental check-ups important?

Many people visit a dentist only when something hurts. While symptoms are an important reason to seek care, preventive dental examinations can also be valuable.

A check-up can help identify problems such as tooth decay, gum inflammation, calculus buildup, damaged fillings or other oral health concerns.

Early identification does not guarantee that treatment will always be simple, but it can allow problems to be assessed before they become more extensive.

  • Detecting dental decay
  • Assessing gum health
  • Identifying calculus and plaque buildup
  • Checking existing fillings, crowns and other restorations
  • Assessing tooth wear or damage
  • Monitoring previously treated teeth
  • Discussing oral hygiene and preventive care

What happens during a dental check-up?

Every appointment is different because your dentist will tailor the examination to your symptoms, history and oral health needs.

1

Medical and dental history

Your dentist may ask about your current concerns, previous dental treatment, medications, allergies and relevant medical or dental history.

2

Examination of the teeth

Your dentist examines the teeth for signs of decay, fractures, wear, discolouration, defective restorations and other concerns.

3

Gum examination

The gums and supporting tissues are assessed for signs such as inflammation, bleeding, recession or other periodontal concerns.

4

Oral soft-tissue examination

Your dentist may also examine the cheeks, tongue, palate and other areas of the mouth for abnormalities that may require further evaluation.

5

X-rays or other investigations when needed

Dental X-rays are not automatically required at every visit. Your dentist may recommend imaging when it is clinically appropriate based on your examination and dental history.

6

Discussion and treatment plan

Your dentist explains the findings and discusses whether you need preventive care, monitoring, professional cleaning or further treatment.

What does a dentist check during an examination?

A dental examination is not limited to looking for cavities. Your dentist considers several aspects of your oral health.

Teeth

Your dentist may look for decay, cracks, fractures, wear, discolouration and problems involving existing fillings or restorations.

Gums

Signs of inflammation, bleeding, recession, periodontal problems and other gum concerns may be assessed.

Plaque and calculus

Your dentist may identify areas where plaque or hardened calculus has accumulated and discuss professional cleaning if appropriate.

Existing dental work

Fillings, crowns, bridges and other restorations may be checked for signs of wear, damage, leakage or other concerns.

Oral tissues

The tongue, cheeks, palate and other soft tissues may be examined for abnormalities that need further assessment.

Will you need an X-ray during a dental check-up?

Not necessarily.

Dental X-rays can provide information about areas that cannot be fully evaluated during a visual examination, such as certain parts of the tooth and surrounding structures.

However, X-rays are generally recommended based on clinical need rather than simply being performed at every appointment.

“I should get an X-ray at every dental visit.”

Not necessarily. The type and timing of dental imaging depend on factors such as your symptoms, examination findings, dental history, age and individual risk.

Is a dental check-up the same as teeth cleaning?

No.

A dental check-up is primarily an examination and assessment of your oral health.

Professional cleaning, such as scaling and polishing, is a separate procedure used to remove plaque, calculus and certain surface stains.

Depending on your needs, a dental appointment may include both an examination and professional cleaning, but they serve different purposes.

How often should you have a dental check-up?

There is no single appointment interval that is appropriate for everyone.

The frequency of dental examinations can depend on factors such as your oral hygiene, history of dental disease, gum health, existing restorations, risk factors and previous dental treatment.

Your dentist can recommend an appropriate follow-up schedule based on your individual needs.

When should you see a dentist sooner?

You do not need to wait for your next routine check-up if you develop a new or concerning symptom.

  • Persistent tooth pain
  • Prolonged sensitivity
  • Swollen or bleeding gums
  • Facial or gum swelling
  • Pain while biting or chewing
  • A broken or cracked tooth
  • A recurring pimple or drainage near the gum
  • A loose adult tooth
  • Persistent bad breath or an unusual taste

These symptoms do not necessarily mean that you have a serious dental problem, but they are reasons to have the area assessed by a dental professional.

What if you are nervous about visiting the dentist?

Dental anxiety is common, and you do not need to feel embarrassed about it.

Tell your dentist that you are nervous before the examination begins. Knowing what is going to happen can make the appointment feel more manageable.

You can also ask your dentist to explain the examination and any proposed treatment before proceeding.

You are allowed to ask questions.

A good dental visit should not feel like a mystery. Ask what your dentist has found, why a treatment is being recommended and what your options are.

Questions you can ask your dentist

If you are unsure about something during your appointment, consider asking:

  • Are my teeth and gums healthy?
  • Do I have any cavities?
  • Do I have calculus or gum inflammation?
  • Do I need professional cleaning?
  • Do I need an X-ray? If so, why?
  • Is any existing filling or crown showing a problem?
  • When should I come back for another examination?
  • What can I improve about my home oral-care routine?

The takeaway

A check-up is about prevention, not just pain.

Regular dental examinations give your dentist an opportunity to assess your teeth, gums and other oral tissues, identify concerns and help you maintain your oral health over time.

You do not have to wait until a tooth hurts before taking your oral health seriously.

If it has been a while since your last dental examination, consider arranging an appointment and discussing your individual dental needs with your dentist.

Common Questions

Dental Check-Up FAQs.

Does a dental check-up hurt?

A routine dental examination is generally not painful. Some areas may feel slightly sensitive if your gums are inflamed or if further procedures are needed, but you can tell your dentist if you experience discomfort.

Can a dentist tell if I have a cavity just by looking?

Some cavities can be identified during a clinical examination, while others may require additional assessment or dental imaging depending on their location and appearance.

Do I need a dental check-up if nothing hurts?

It can still be useful. Some dental and gum problems may develop without obvious symptoms, especially in their earlier stages.

Is professional cleaning always done during a check-up?

Not necessarily. Examination and professional cleaning are different procedures. Your dentist can recommend cleaning if plaque, calculus or other deposits indicate that it would be beneficial.

What should I tell my dentist during a check-up?

Tell your dentist about any pain, sensitivity, bleeding, swelling, changes in your mouth, previous dental treatment, medications or other concerns that may be relevant to your oral health.
