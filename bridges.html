Tooth Replacement

Dental bridges,
explained simply.

A dental bridge is one option for replacing a missing tooth or several missing teeth. It uses supporting structures to hold replacement teeth in the space created by missing teeth.

What is a dental bridge?

A dental bridge is a fixed dental restoration used to replace one or more missing teeth. The replacement tooth or teeth are connected to supporting structures, which may include natural teeth or dental implants.

Unlike a removable denture, a conventional fixed bridge is designed to remain in place rather than being removed by the patient.

Why might a dental bridge be recommended?

A missing tooth can affect chewing, appearance and the way neighbouring teeth interact with each other.

Depending on the individual situation, a bridge may be considered as one option for restoring the missing tooth or teeth.

A bridge is not the only option.

Depending on the number and position of missing teeth, the condition of the surrounding teeth and gums, bone support and other factors, options may include a bridge, dental implant or removable denture.

What are the different types of bridges?

Traditional bridge

A traditional bridge is supported by crowns placed on teeth next to the space where the tooth is missing.

Cantilever bridge

A cantilever bridge is supported from one side rather than both sides. Whether this type is appropriate depends on the location and forces involved.

Maryland bridge

A Maryland bridge uses a framework or wings that attach to the back surfaces of supporting teeth. It may be considered in selected situations.

Implant-supported bridge

An implant-supported bridge uses dental implants as support instead of relying entirely on natural teeth.

What happens when getting a bridge?

Step 01

Assessment

Your dentist examines the missing tooth area and evaluates the surrounding teeth and tissues.

Step 02

Preparation

If a conventional bridge is planned, the supporting teeth may need to be prepared.

Step 03

Placement

The completed bridge is fitted and checked for comfort, function and appearance.

How do you care for a dental bridge?

Good oral hygiene remains important after getting a bridge. Brushing twice a day and cleaning around and underneath the bridge helps maintain the surrounding teeth and gums.

Your dentist or hygienist can demonstrate suitable cleaning techniques, which may include floss threaders, interdental brushes or other cleaning aids depending on the bridge design.

The supporting teeth matter too.

A bridge depends on its supporting structures. Keeping the supporting teeth and surrounding gums healthy is an important part of maintaining the restoration.

Is a dental bridge right for you?

There is no single tooth-replacement option that is suitable for everyone. Your dentist will consider the location of the missing tooth, the condition of nearby teeth and gums, your bite, bone support and your overall oral health before recommending an option.

A proper examination is therefore important before deciding whether a bridge is appropriate.
