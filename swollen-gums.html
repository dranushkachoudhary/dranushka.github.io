Gum Health

“Doctor, Why Are My Gums Swollen?”

Swollen gums can feel uncomfortable, look different from usual and sometimes bleed when you brush. But what actually causes them to swell?

By Dr. Anushka Choudhary · August 2026 · 5 min read
“Doctor, my gums look puffy and swollen. Is this normal?”

Swollen gums are not usually something to simply ignore. They can have several causes, and the right approach depends on what is causing the inflammation.

What do healthy gums normally look like?

Healthy gums generally fit closely around the teeth and are usually firm rather than noticeably puffy or swollen.

The exact colour of healthy gums can vary between individuals, so colour alone cannot determine whether gums are healthy.

Changes such as swelling, redness, tenderness or bleeding can be signs that the gum tissues are irritated or inflamed.

The most common cause: plaque

One of the most common reasons gums become swollen is the buildup of plaque.

Plaque is a sticky layer containing bacteria that forms on teeth throughout the day.

When plaque is not removed effectively, the bacteria can irritate the gums.

This can lead to gingivitis, an inflammation of the gum tissues.

Swollen gums are often your body's response to irritation around the teeth.

Could brushing too hard cause swollen gums?

Brushing harder does not mean brushing better.

Aggressive brushing or using an unsuitable brushing technique can irritate the gums.

A soft-bristled toothbrush and gentle technique are generally preferable to forceful scrubbing.

If your gums remain swollen despite improving your brushing technique, there may be another underlying cause that needs assessment.

What about flossing?

Cleaning between your teeth is an important part of oral hygiene because a toothbrush cannot effectively reach every surface between the teeth.

If plaque is accumulating between your teeth, the gums in those areas may become irritated and swollen.

Gentle and consistent interdental cleaning can help remove plaque from these difficult-to-reach areas.

If you are unsure about your technique, a dentist or dental professional can show you how to clean between your teeth effectively.

Could tartar be causing the problem?

Plaque can harden into calculus, commonly called tartar.

Calculus cannot simply be removed with normal brushing at home.

It can contribute to plaque retention and make keeping the gums clean more difficult.

When calculus has accumulated, professional dental cleaning may be needed.

Could swollen gums be related to gum disease?

Yes.

Gingivitis causes inflammation of the gums and may result in swelling, redness and bleeding.

If gum inflammation progresses and affects the deeper tissues supporting the teeth, it can develop into periodontitis.

Periodontitis can damage the tissues and bone that support teeth and therefore needs professional management.

Why is only one area of my gum swollen?

Swelling does not always affect the entire mouth.

A localized swollen area can have different possible causes, including plaque accumulation, food trapped around a tooth, irritation or a problem involving a particular tooth.

If the swelling is painful, repeatedly returns or is associated with pus or tooth pain, it should be assessed by a dentist.

Can a wisdom tooth cause swollen gums?

Yes.

When a wisdom tooth is partially erupted, the gum around it can sometimes become inflamed.

Food and bacteria may become trapped around the area, making it difficult to keep clean.

This can result in pain, swelling, unpleasant taste or difficulty opening the mouth.

A dentist can examine the area and determine whether the wisdom tooth is contributing to the problem.

Can hormonal changes affect the gums?

Hormonal changes can influence the way gum tissues respond to plaque.

This can make some people more susceptible to gum inflammation during certain hormonal changes, including pregnancy.

Good oral hygiene and appropriate dental care remain important during these periods.

When should swollen gums concern you?

Swollen gums deserve particular attention if they occur together with other symptoms.

  • Persistent gum bleeding
  • Significant gum pain or tenderness
  • Pus around the gums
  • Persistent bad breath or unpleasant taste
  • Tooth pain or sensitivity
  • Gum recession
  • Loose or shifting teeth
  • Facial or jaw swelling

These signs can have different causes, but they are reasons to seek professional dental evaluation rather than waiting indefinitely for the swelling to disappear.

What can you do at home?

Start by focusing on gentle and consistent oral hygiene.

  • Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and avoid aggressive scrubbing.
  • Clean between your teeth regularly.
  • Gently clean your tongue.
  • Stay adequately hydrated.
  • Arrange a dental examination if swelling persists or keeps returning.

Should you stop brushing swollen gums?

Usually, stopping oral hygiene completely is not the answer.

If plaque is contributing to the inflammation, avoiding the area can allow more plaque to accumulate.

Instead, aim for gentle cleaning rather than aggressive brushing.

Swollen gums need gentle care — not neglect and not aggressive scrubbing.

What will a dentist check?

Your dentist may examine the gums, teeth and areas around the swelling.

They may look for plaque, calculus, gum inflammation, periodontal pockets, tooth decay, infection or other possible causes.

If necessary, dental X-rays or other investigations may be recommended depending on your symptoms and examination.

One thing I wish more patients knew

Swollen gums are easy to dismiss because they may not always hurt significantly.

But the absence of severe pain does not automatically mean that everything is fine.

Your gums are part of the tissues that support your teeth, so persistent changes deserve attention.

Don't just ask, “How do I make the swelling go down?”

Also ask: “What is causing my gums to swell?”

The takeaway

Swollen gums can have several causes, but plaque-related inflammation is one of the most common.

Gentle brushing, regular interdental cleaning and appropriate professional dental care can help maintain healthy gums.

If swelling persists, repeatedly returns or occurs with bleeding, pus, tooth pain, loose teeth or facial swelling, arrange a dental assessment.

Healthy gums shouldn't have to be painful, swollen or constantly bleeding for you to notice them.
